The November 6 LibTV ledger shows 60 credits for five seconds of Wan 3.0. The next day we checked two five-second, 1080p text-to-video nodes on the canvas. One had sound, one didn’t, and both showed 80 credits. We don’t have enough settings from the earlier job to explain the difference. Both figures appear in the sequence estimate below.

These notes cover November 5–16, 2026. The LibTV charge of 60 credits comes from the ledger. The tables use generation quotes, published rates or estimates from subscription prices as indicated.

## Three quotes for the same five-second shot

For the comparison we used Wan 3.0 image-to-video, five seconds, sound on. On November 6 we checked the OpenArt and Renoise estimators without submitting either job. We used their regular Starter monthly prices to convert credits to US dollars. fal publishes its rate in dollars per second.

| Platform and billing | 720p · five seconds | 1080p · five seconds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fal · per second | $0.50 | $1.00 |
| OpenArt · Starter monthly | 200 credits ≈ $0.70 | 400 credits ≈ $1.40 |
| Renoise · Starter monthly | 60 credits ≈ $1.00 | 120 credits ≈ $2.00 |

Renoise’s 120 credits cost more than OpenArt’s 400 here. On Renoise, $20 bought 1,200 monthly credits. On OpenArt, $14 bought 4,000. The $2 and $1.40 in the table assume the whole allowance gets used. Someone subscribing for this shot alone would still pay $20 or $14. [fal model rates](https://fal.ai/models/alibaba/wan-3.0/image-to-video) · [OpenArt plans](https://openart.ai/pricing) · [Renoise plans](https://renoise.ai/pricing)

## Two motion tests and one final take

Suppose the first two five-second takes are 720p motion tests and the third is 1080p. That comes to $2 on fal, 800 credits (about $2.80) on OpenArt and 240 credits (about $4) on Renoise. Three 1080p takes would cost $3, $4.20 and $6 respectively.

A 720p take may tell you when the actor turns. To see what happens to the face as it turns, budget a take at delivery resolution. Generating the shot again at 1080p can change the expression.

## Converting subscriptions to dollars

For dollars per second, divide the monthly price by the credit allowance, then multiply by credits per second. Annual subscriptions use one twelfth of the annual bill. The low end of a range often requires buying a large annual allowance and using it all.

| OpenArt · Wonder–Starter annual | $0.13–0.26 | $0.21–0.42 | $0.53–1.04 |
| Flick · Studio annual–Standard monthly | $0.10–0.19 | $0.20–0.38 | ≈$0.45–0.86 |
| Frameo · Pro–Basic monthly | $0.35–0.47 | $0.35–0.47 | $0.90–1.20 |
| Renoise · Advanced–Starter monthly | $0.30–0.40 | $0.40–0.53 | $0.90–1.20 |
| Higgsfield · Ultra–Starter annual | $0.12–0.25 | — | — |
| Krea · plan allocation | — | ≈$0.18–0.30 | ≈$0.44–0.74 |

OpenArt’s annual plans ranged from $13 a month for 4,000 credits to $175 for 106,000. For Flick we used Studio annual ($100 a month, 24,000 credits) and Standard monthly ($20, 2,500). For Frameo we used Basic ($10, 600) and Pro ($100, 8,000), both monthly. Renoise Advanced came to 16,000 credits for $200 including the bonus. Pollo displayed only ¥, so we couldn’t tell which currency to convert.

For 1080p Kling 4, the same allocation gives OpenArt about $0.30–0.59 per second, Frameo $0.36–0.48 and Renoise $0.48–0.63. Kling’s own API quoted 1 / 1.5 units per second at 720p / 1080p without video input. With video input, 0.6 / 0.9 units apply to at least twice the output duration. No verified dollar price per unit was available.

## Scaling a test to a sequence

These durations are footage to generate. Two takes of 34 seconds cost 68 seconds. Frameo’s November 12 Wan 3.0 quotes were 7, 14 and 28 credits per second at 480p, 720p and 1080p. For LibTV we calculated each duration twice, using 12 and 16 credits per second from the five-second records at the start.

| Generated footage | Frameo 480p / 720p / 1080p | LibTV 1080p scenarios |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 34 s | 238 / 476 / 952 | 408–544 |
| 171 s | 1,197 / 2,394 / 4,788 | 2,052–2,736 |
| 627 s | 4,389 / 8,778 / 17,556 | 7,524–10,032 |

LibTV’s recorded monthly plan worked out to roughly CNY 0.04 per credit. That puts 34 seconds at CNY 16–22 and 171 seconds at CNY 82–109. A single pass over all 627 seconds comes to CNY 301–401. A retake adds the duration of that shot.

## Flick TV: audience tips can fund the next film

On November 18 we checked Flick TV’s publishing and tipping features. You can publish a finished film there, and viewers who enjoy it can support you with credits. Received tips never expire. Generation uses your other credits first, then your tips. For a new filmmaker on a small budget, that support can pay for another shot or go toward the next film. [Publishing a film](https://flick.art/docs/publish-your-film) · [Tipping](https://flick.art/docs/tips)

Eligible tips can instead be withdrawn at 250 credits per US dollar once the cashable balance reaches $20, subject to review. Tips funded by free or promotional credits may not qualify for withdrawal but can pay for generation. Credits already spent cannot also be cashed out. The tables above keep the original generation costs; record tips separately as they arrive, are spent or are withdrawn to see how much production spending they actually cover. [Cash-out rules](https://flick.art/docs/cash-outs)
